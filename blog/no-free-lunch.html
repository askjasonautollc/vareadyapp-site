What to ask before you trust a free VA claims tool

A free VA claims tool has to be paid for somehow. Common answers are your data, an investor subsidy, or a cheaper model. Questions to ask before you share your claim.

VA Ready App · June 14, 2026 · 4 min read

Plenty of free tools offer help with a VA disability claim: calculators, condition checkers, "find a rep" directories, AI assistants that answer your questions. Free is good. Veterans have earned help that costs nothing.

Before you give any of them your information, ask one question: if it's free, how does it stay running?

Servers, data, development and upkeep cost real money, and some tools carry a cost most people never think about. Someone pays that bill. If you are not paying it with money, find out how it gets paid, because the answer affects you.

Here are the two most common ways a free claims tool gets funded, and what each means for the veteran using it.

1. Your data is the product

When a tool is free and asks you to create an account, log in, and hand over your name, your email and details about your service and your claim, pause.

A veteran who is separating and filing a disability claim is, statistically, about to make some of the biggest financial decisions of their life: a VA home loan, insurance, maybe legal or financial services. Your name, your email and the fact that you are filing a claim are a valuable lead to a lot of businesses.

A free tool that collects that information can sell it, route it to partners or market to you. Sometimes that is disclosed up front. More often it sits in a privacy policy nobody reads. Plenty of free tools are built by good people, so this is no charge against all of them. But you learned to ask who benefits and to read the fine print before you sign. Apply that here.

Questions to ask before you trust a free tool:

  • Does it make you create an account and log in before you can use it? If a tool needs your email and a profile before it shows you a calculator, ask what it needs that information for.
  • What does the privacy policy say? Look for "share," "third parties," "partners" and "marketing." Those words tell you where your data can travel.
  • Who owns it, and what else do they sell? A free veteran tool run by a company whose business is loans, insurance or legal services tells you how the free part gets funded.
  • Can you use it without giving anything up? The best answer to "where is my data going" is "nowhere," because the tool never collected it.

2. Who pays for a free AI tool

Every time an "Ask the AI" tool answers a question, the operator pays the AI company that runs the model, per use. A calculator costs the same whether ten people or ten thousand use it. With AI, the meter runs on every question. The more veterans use a free AI tool, the more it costs the people running it.

Nobody absorbs that cost out of goodwill forever, so ask who covers it. Investors may subsidize the AI to grow, then add a paywall or raise the price once people are hooked, and a veteran who built a whole claim workflow around the free tool loses it when the funding pressure hits. Your conversation may cover it: everything you type about your claim, your conditions and your situation makes a far richer profile than an email address, and the cost of the AI calls can be the price of collecting better data to sell. Or the operator runs the cheapest model, and cheaper models make things up more often. On a VA claim that can mean invented citations and confident, wrong answers, and a wrong answer can cost you the claim.

What VA Ready does about it

We made two choices, and both come from the questions above.

No accounts, no logins, no profile. Your conditions, your claim list and your service details stay on your device and never touch a server we control. We do not collect them, so we cannot sell, share or lose them. When you ask where your data is going, the answer is nowhere.

No AI guessing at your evidence. The research in VA Ready is peer-reviewed medical literature, verified against the published record and mapped to your conditions. We do not run an AI model that costs us per question and tempts us to cut quality to afford it, and we do not hand you citations a machine invented. The studies are verified once and served to everyone.

We charge a few dollars for Pro. The calculator, the VSO finder and the core features are free, with no strings. You pay a fair price or use the free tools, and either way your data stays yours and your evidence is real.

A fair transaction means you know what you are paying for and what you are getting. Ask that of any tool that wants your claim.
