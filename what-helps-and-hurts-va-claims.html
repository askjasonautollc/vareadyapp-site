What helps and hurts a VA claim, according to Board decisions

We read thousands of decisions of the Board of Veterans’ Appeals across 171 conditions and kept the patterns that repeat. Here is what the Board pointed to when it helped a veteran’s claim, and what it pointed to when it did not.

From Board of Veterans’ Appeals decisions. Cases differ. These are patterns, not odds.

How to read this page

Each pattern below showed up in at least three different Board decisions, usually on many conditions. We list the Board’s own reasoning in plain words, not a prediction. A decision that sent a claim back for a new exam is not a win, and we say so where it applies. The most-claimed conditions have a page with every finding and the decision numbers; the others are in the VA Ready app.

What helped claims

  1. VA negative opinions lost weight when thin
  2. A reasoned doctor opinion linked it to service
  3. Credible personal accounts were accepted
  4. Sent back for a new exam or opinion
  5. Records from service showed the problem
  6. Secondary link to a service-connected condition
  7. Exposure was conceded or presumed
  8. Close evidence went to the veteran
  9. Exams that missed flare-ups or pain were faulted
  10. A current diagnosis was confirmed

VA negative opinions lost weight when thin

The Board gave little weight to VA opinions that skipped records, ignored the veteran's account, used wrong facts or gave no reasons.

Seen on 106 conditions.

  • Negative VA opinions lost weight when they skipped the theory, literature or full record.Chronic renal disease requiring regular dialysis
  • Negative VA opinions lost weight when based on wrong facts or ignoring the veteran's accountSpondylolisthesis or segmental instability
  • A negative VA opinion lost weight when it leaned on silent service records.Tinnitus, recurrent
  • Negative VA opinions that were conclusory or skipped key evidence lost weight.Thyroid enlargement, toxic
  • VA negative opinions built on wrong facts or missed questions got little or no weightMajor depressive disorder

A reasoned doctor opinion linked it to service

Private or treating doctors who gave reasons, cited records or studies, and tied the condition to service carried weight with the Board.

Seen on 69 conditions.

  • Private doctor opinions that reviewed the file and cited medical studies got strong weight.Major or mild neurocognitive disorder due to Alzheimer's disease
  • Board gave high weight to private opinions that cited medical studies and explained the link.Multiple myeloma
  • Favorable opinion giving a stated reason, such as service activity or medical literature, was credited.Bursitis
  • Doctor opinions tying gastritis to NSAID use for service-connected conditions were accepted.Gastritis, chronic
  • Private opinions with full record review and written reasoning drew significant weight from the Board.Schizophrenia

Credible personal accounts were accepted

The Board accepted believable accounts of symptoms that began in service and continued, especially when records fit the story.

Seen on 66 conditions.

  • Credible veteran accounts of when joint symptoms began were accepted.Multi-Joint Arthritis (Active Process, 2+ Joints)
  • Credible personal account of eye problems since service, consistent with records, was accepted.Pterygium
  • Veteran's own report of dry eyes starting in service and continuing was accepted.Disorders of the lacrimal apparatus (epiphora, dacryocystitis, etc.)
  • Consistent veteran accounts of back injury and pain since service were found credibleSpondylolisthesis or segmental instability
  • Credible accounts from the veteran and family of stomach symptoms in service and after helped.Crohn's disease or undifferentiated form of inflammatory bowel disease

Sent back for a new exam or opinion

When VA gave no exam, or an exam that skipped a theory, a symptom or the records, the Board sent the claim back instead of deciding it.

Seen on 92 conditions.

  • Sent back when the VA examiner ignored the veteran's account or gave no reasons.Gout
  • Sent back for a new opinion when VA's opinion was conclusory or skipped a theory.Supraventricular tachycardia
  • Sent back for new opinions when VA examiners skipped a secondary or direct theory.Bronchiectasis
  • Sent back for an opinion when no examiner addressed a possible secondary link.Aortic aneurysm: Ascending, thoracic, or abdominal
  • Claims were sent back when VA examiners did not address aggravation by a service-connected condition.Erectile dysfunction, with or without penile deformity

Records from service showed the problem

Treatment notes, complaints or diagnoses recorded during service supported the in-service part of the claim.

Seen on 36 conditions.

  • Service records showing foot or toe complaints or treatment supported the claim.Hammer toe
  • Eye injury or diagnosis recorded during service backed the later claim.Pterygium
  • Service records showing gynecologic treatment during service supported the grants.Uterus, removal of, including corpus
  • Service records showing a rash or eczema supported finding an in-service start.Dermatitis or eczema
  • Treatment records showing chest pain or costochondritis in service supported the claim.Costochondritis (chest wall pain)

Secondary link to a service-connected condition

Medical opinions linking a new condition to one already service connected, by cause or by making it worse, supported secondary grants.

Seen on 45 conditions.

  • A clinician tied glaucoma to a service-connected condition or its treatment.Angle-closure glaucoma
  • A heart attack tied to a service-connected mental health condition or hypertension was granted.Myocardial infarction
  • Panic disorder was granted as secondary to another condition when the evidence was balanced.Panic disorder and/or agoraphobia
  • Anemia tied by a medical opinion to a service-connected kidney or blood conditionAnemia, hypochromic-microcytic and megaloblastic
  • Reasoned opinions tying finger or thumb problems to duties or a service-connected condition carried weight.Index or long finger, limitation of motion

Exposure was conceded or presumed

Where service location or records supported exposure to herbicides, burn pits or similar hazards, the Board accepted exposure and the case turned on the medical link.

Seen on 30 conditions.

  • Verified Southwest Asia service plus a current rhinitis diagnosis supported presumptive service connectionAllergic or vasomotor rhinitis
  • Credible accounts of work near a base perimeter or the DMZ established exposure.Malignant neoplasms of the genitourinary system
  • Conceded or presumed herbicide exposure, from service location or a VA memo, supported the claim.Leukemia (except for chronic myelogenous leukemia)
  • Conceded Gulf region service or toxic exposure supported grants of lung disease.Emphysema, pulmonary
  • Board linked thyroid disease to conceded herbicide or toxic exposure when medical evidence supported it.Thyroid enlargement, toxic

Close evidence went to the veteran

When medical opinions or records were evenly balanced, the Board gave the veteran the benefit of the doubt.

Seen on 22 conditions.

  • Evidence on stroke cause found evenly balanced, so doubt went to the veteranBrain, vessels, embolism of
  • Positive medical opinions balanced the evidence, and doubt was resolved for the veteran.Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease
  • Competing medical opinions left the Board at even balance, and the claim was granted.Chronic liver disease without cirrhosis
  • Favorable medical opinions, private or VA, supported grants, including when opinions were evenly balanced.Prostate Conditions (Injury, Infection, BPH, Postop)
  • Positive opinions with reasoning, or split opinions, put foot claims in balance.Tarsal, or metatarsal bones, malunion of, or nonunion of

Exams that missed flare-ups or pain were faulted

Exams that left out flare-ups, repeated use or medication effects were found inadequate, and many of those claims were sent back for a new exam.

Seen on 14 conditions.

  • Sent back for a new exam when it missed flare-up or medication effects.Index or long finger, limitation of motion
  • Sent back for a new exam when the examiner skipped flare-ups, bleeding or medication effects.Hemorrhoids, external or internal
  • Exams missing weight-bearing, passive or flare-up testing were inadequate; some claims sent back.Leg, limitation of extension of
  • Back exams that ignored flare-ups or repeated-use effects were sent back for a new examSpondylolisthesis or segmental instability
  • Exams missing flare-up findings were found inadequate and the claim was sent back.Bursitis

A current diagnosis was confirmed

An exam, treatment record or private diagnosis showing the condition during the claim met the current-disability question.

Seen on 11 conditions.

  • A diagnosis in a VA exam or treatment record met the current-condition question.Osteoporosis, residuals of
  • A skin exam or treatment record naming alopecia met the current-disability question.Alopecia areata
  • Diagnosis shown by lab tests, records or a VA exam met the current disability element.Leukemia (except for chronic myelogenous leukemia)
  • A formal OCD diagnosis from a licensed clinician met the current-disability requirement.Obsessive compulsive disorder
  • A VA exam confirming lipomas or keratosis established the current diagnosis.Benign skin neoplasms

What hurt claims

  1. Service records were silent
  2. No current diagnosis was found
  3. The veteran's own belief was not enough
  4. Years passed before the first diagnosis or care
  5. Reasoned VA opinions against a link carried weight
  6. No doctor tied it to service
  7. The claimed exposure was not shown
  8. Opinions without reasons got little weight
  9. Earlier records contradicted the veteran's account

Service records were silent

Records from service that showed no complaint, injury or diagnosis, or a normal separation exam, weighed against the claim.

Seen on 84 conditions.

  • Service treatment records silent on thyroid problems weighed against the claim.Thyroid enlargement, toxic
  • Service records with no GERD complaint or diagnosis weighed against a link.Hiatal hernia and paraesophageal hernia
  • Quiet service records and a clean separation exam weighed against in-service shin injury.Tibia and fibula, impairment of
  • Service records silent on anemia, with first diagnosis years after dischargeIron deficiency anemia
  • Service treatment records and separation exam said nothing about the hipsThigh, limitation of flexion of

No current diagnosis was found

Exams and records that showed no diagnosis during the claim period ended the claim at the first step.

Seen on 58 conditions.

  • Records with no diagnosis of a tendon condition left the claim without a current disability.Tenosynovitis, tendinitis, tendinosis, tendinopathy
  • Claims failed where VA exams or records showed no vertigo diagnosis, despite dizziness complaints.Peripheral vestibular disorders
  • No current diagnosis of cold injury residuals in exams or recordsCold injury residuals
  • No current hemorrhoid diagnosis in treatment records or exams ended the service claim.Hemorrhoids, external or internal
  • No current rhinitis diagnosis in the records ended the service connection claimAllergic or vasomotor rhinitis

The veteran's own belief was not enough

Personal views on diagnosis or cause were not accepted as medical evidence, because those are medical questions.

Seen on 73 conditions.

  • The veteran's own view that service caused the ringing was not acceptedTinnitus, recurrent
  • The veteran's own view of the liver cause carried little weight without medical backing.Chronic liver disease without cirrhosis
  • The veteran's own belief about the cause of migraines was not accepted as medical proofMigraine
  • A veteran's own belief about foot diagnosis or cause got little weight against medical evidence.Foot injuries, other
  • Veteran or family belief that service caused dementia was not competent evidence of cause.Major or mild neurocognitive disorder due to Alzheimer's disease

Years passed before the first diagnosis or care

Long gaps between service and the first complaint, treatment or diagnosis weighed against a link to service.

Seen on 43 conditions.

  • Long gaps between service and first back treatment weighed against a link to serviceSpondylolisthesis or segmental instability
  • Silent service records and respiratory records first appearing decades later weighed against the claim.Bronchiectasis
  • Heart disease first diagnosed decades after service, with silent service records, weighed against the claim.Myocardial infarction
  • Arthritis first appearing in records decades after service weighed against the claim.Degenerative arthritis, other than post-traumatic
  • A first diagnosis long after service, with normal earlier records, weakened the claim.Diverticulitis and diverticulosis

Reasoned VA opinions against a link carried weight

VA opinions that reviewed the file and explained why the condition was not linked to service outweighed competing views.

Seen on 26 conditions.

  • Negative VA opinions citing medical literature and other stroke risk factors were given high weightBrain, vessels, embolism of
  • A negative VA opinion with clear reasons, often a long smoking history, was adopted.Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease
  • Negative VA opinions with explained reasoning were found probative against a service link.Diverticulitis and diverticulosis
  • Negative VA opinions with a rationale, literature and an accurate history were found persuasive.Malignant skin neoplasms (other than malignant melanoma)
  • Explained negative VA opinion with no opposing medical opinion weighed against the claimChronic biliary tract disease

No doctor tied it to service

Without a medical opinion linking the condition or exposure to service, the link was left unsupported.

Seen on 16 conditions.

  • No medical opinion linking kidney disease to service left direct claims unsupported.Chronic renal disease requiring regular dialysis
  • Board found no medical opinion or evidence linking melanoma to the reported exposure.Malignant melanoma
  • Conceded herbicide or toxic exposure did not carry the claim without a medical linkPeripheral arterial disease
  • No medical opinion tied HIV to service, so the claim fell short.HIV-related illness
  • No medical opinion linked the eye disease to service.Retinal Dystrophy (Macular Degeneration, Retinitis Pigmentosa)

The claimed exposure was not shown

Records that did not place the veteran in a covered place, or did not confirm the exposure, left the exposure theory unproven.

Seen on 16 conditions.

  • Herbicide exposure was not accepted when service location or dates fell outside the presumption.Respiratory System Cancer (Malignant)
  • Service outside the listed places and periods meant exposure was not presumed.Malignant neoplasms of the genitourinary system
  • Herbicide exposure stayed unproven when records showed no service in a listed location.Paralysis agitans
  • Records did not show herbicide exposure, so the heart claim failed on that theory.Coronary bypass surgery
  • Herbicide or toxic exposure claimed for a stroke was not shown by the record.Brain, vessels, thrombosis of

Opinions without reasons got little weight

Opinions, letters or notes with no stated reasons, no record review or no clinical findings were given little or no weight.

Seen on 14 conditions.

  • A private opinion with no stated reasons or no examination carried little weightAllergic or vasomotor rhinitis
  • Private opinions without full reasoning were found inadequate or given no weight.Aortic aneurysm: Ascending, thoracic, or abdominal
  • Favorable opinions with no rationale, or built on the veteran account alone, were discounted.Multi-Joint Arthritis (Active Process, 2+ Joints)
  • Private opinions got little weight when they lacked reasoning or skipped the period at issue.Ulcer, gastric
  • A positive opinion with no explanation, or built on general articles, got little weightMigraine

Earlier records contradicted the veteran's account

Accounts of continuous symptoms lost weight when older records, separation paperwork or earlier histories said something different.

Seen on 9 conditions.

  • Later claims of constant pain were not believed when older records said otherwiseDegenerative Disc Disease / Arthritis (Spine)
  • Later claims of foot symptoms since service lost credibility when earlier histories said otherwise.Foot injuries, other
  • Claims of pain since service lost weight when contemporaneous records showed no back complaintsSpondylolisthesis or segmental instability
  • Separation paperwork where the veteran denied ear trouble was weighed against the claimTinnitus, recurrent
  • A veteran's account of in-service onset was outweighed when records did not corroborate itRotator Cuff Muscle Injury (Group IV)

Questions

Where do these patterns come from?

From decisions of the Board of Veterans' Appeals, which VA publishes. For 171 conditions we read the decisions and kept only patterns that showed up in at least three different decisions. Every finding in the app lists the decision numbers behind it.

Does a pattern here mean my claim will be granted or denied?

No. These are patterns in past decisions, not odds. Each case turns on its own records, and the Board decides on the whole file.

What does a sent-back decision mean?

The Board returned the claim for a new exam or medical opinion. It is not a grant, and it is not a denial. It means the earlier exam or opinion was not enough to decide.

Why do VA opinions that skip your records lose weight?

Across many conditions the Board found negative VA opinions inadequate when they ignored the veteran's account, relied on missing records, gave no reasons or used wrong facts. Opinions with a clear reason tied to the record carried weight.

Where can I see the findings for my condition?

Is this legal advice?

No. VA Ready is not a law firm and is not affiliated with the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs. For help with a claim, an accredited VSO is free.
