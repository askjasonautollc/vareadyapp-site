A Veteran told us this week that finding his service treatment records has been the hardest part of his claim, and that he cannot find anyone who knows where they are. He is not alone. Here is where to look, in order.

## What service treatment records are

Service treatment records, or STRs, are the medical records created while you were on active duty: sick call notes, exams, immunizations, injuries and hospital stays. They matter because a claim asks the VA to connect a condition to your service, and your STRs often show when a problem first appeared.

## Step 1: Request them from the National Personnel Records Center

The National Personnel Records Center (NPRC), part of the National Archives in St. Louis, holds records for most Veterans who have left service. You have two ways to ask:

- **Online with eVetRecs.** You verify your identity through ID.me.
- **By mail or fax with Standard Form 180 (SF-180).** The form and the current address and fax number are on the National Archives page linked below. Check them there before you send anything, because they can change.

Basic military personnel and medical record information is generally free to Veterans.

## Step 2: Ask for the hospital records separately

Inpatient (clinical) treatment records are filed apart from your main medical file. The National Archives recommends NA Form 13042 for these, and it lists what to include. If you ever filed a medical-related VA claim, the VA may already hold some of your clinical records, so ask your VSO what is in your file before you pay for a second search.

## Step 3: Give it time

The National Archives asks you to wait 90 days before you send a follow-up request. Put the date on your calendar when you submit.

## If your records were lost or burned

A 1973 fire at the NPRC destroyed a large number of Army and Air Force records. If yours were among them, the VA can ask the NPRC to reconstruct them using NA Form 13055. The VA also suggests gathering private hospital or clinic records and working with an accredited Veterans Service Officer, attorney or claims agent. Buddy statements and your own dated notes can help fill gaps. See our guide on [buddy statements](/guides/buddy-statements.html).

## Do not wait on the records to start

Records can take months. If you plan to file, ask a VSO about an [intent to file](/guides/intent-to-file.html), which can protect your start date while the paperwork arrives. Also ask whether the VA can request your STRs as part of your claim. The VA says it requests your DD214 for you when you apply, and the VA page we checked did not say the same about STRs, so confirm it with your VSO. You can find free, accredited help on our [VSO finder](/find-a-vso.html).

## Keep track of what you have

Whatever you collect, write down where each page lives. VA Ready's evidence checklist (a Pro feature) tracks service records, diagnosis, nexus letter, buddy statements, medical records and DBQ for each condition, and lets you note the page number, so your VSO can go straight to it.

This is general information, not legal advice. Every claim is decided on its own evidence, and nothing here promises a rating or a timeline.
