Type "VA back pay calculator" into a search bar and look at what comes back. Law firms. Lead-generation sites. "Free evaluation" forms that want your name, your email, your phone number and your rating before they show you a single number.

Back pay is the one place a fee can legally come from, so it is where fee-seekers gather. Once you understand that, you can walk past the hook.

## Follow the money

When the VA grants or increases a rating, it usually owes you a lump sum for the time between your effective date and the day the decision landed. That is your back pay, also called retroactive pay or "retro." It is a one-time check, and it can be large.

Most veterans are never told this part. Under federal law, an accredited attorney or claims agent can only charge a fee that is a percentage of your past-due (retroactive) benefits. Fees at or under 20 percent of that retro are presumed reasonable, and fees over 33 and a third percent are presumed unreasonable ([38 CFR 14.636(e)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/38/14.636)). The VA withholds that cut directly out of the retro lump sum and pays the representative before you ever see it (14.636(h)).

The fee is a slice of your back pay. It is not a slice of your monthly check going forward, and it is not a flat rate. It is a percentage of that one big retroactive number.

If you were building a business around VA-claim fees, you would point every ad, every calculator and every "see what you are owed" button at the back pay. That is the entire prize.

## Three rules that cut through the noise

1. No one can legally charge you to file your original claim. Not an attorney, not a claims agent, and not an unaccredited "coach." Under [38 CFR 14.636(c)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/38/14.636), a fee can only be charged after the VA has issued its first decision on the claim. Charging you to fill out and file the initial claim is not allowed.
2. Only VA-accredited people can represent you for a fee. The VA accredits attorneys, claims agents and Veterans Service Organization (VSO) representatives under [38 USC 5901](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/38/5901). Everyone else is operating outside the system.
3. VSO help is free, all of it. A VA-accredited VSO can prepare and file your claim, including your very first one, at no cost. The VA says so on its own [find-a-representative page](https://www.va.gov/get-help-from-accredited-representative/).

## Where it goes wrong

The gap between those rules and what happens in practice is where veterans get hurt.

In 2023, the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau and the VA jointly warned that unaccredited individuals and organizations were aggressively soliciting veterans and pushing them to sign contracts for a percentage of their retroactive or future benefits in exchange for help preparing initial claims, which is help that cannot legally be charged for. The agencies pointed to veterans paying substantial illegal fees. These outfits often call themselves "coaches" or "consultants" precisely because they are not accredited.

A common pricing model reported across veteran journalism is a fee equal to several times your first month of increased pay, collected up front, for help you could have gotten free. It is sold as a service and works as a cut of your back pay.

States have started pushing back. In June 2026 a court entered a consent judgment in Arizona's case against VetLink Solutions, which the attorney general said charged veterans contingency fees for VA claim help without accreditation; the company paid $1.2 million into a consumer restitution fund. Texas's attorney general won a judgment against VA Claims Insider that cancels an estimated $6.8 million in debts veterans owed the company. In Congress, the GUARD VA Benefits Act (H.R. 1732, 119th Congress) would restore criminal penalties for unaccredited actors who charge illegal fees, penalties that were stripped from the law about twenty years ago. As of this writing it sits in committee, bipartisan but not yet passed.

## Red flags

- Someone wants a percentage of your back pay to file your original claim.
- They cannot show you a VA accreditation number. You can verify one yourself with the [VA's accreditation search](https://www.va.gov/get-help-from-accredited-representative/find-rep/).
- They "guarantee" a rating or a dollar amount. Only the VA decides ratings.
- The calculator will not show you a number until you hand over your contact information.

Any one of those is your cue to slow down.
