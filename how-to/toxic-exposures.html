How to check toxic exposures and PACT Act presumptives

A presumptive condition skips the hardest part of a claim: proving the link to service. Add where you served and VA Ready matches 834 bases and 148 airframes against 56 exposures, then lists the presumptive conditions with the window and the authority for each.

Where it is in the app: Claim Intel tab, Exposures view. Part of VA Ready Pro.

Steps

  1. Open the Claim Intel tab and switch to Exposures.
  2. Add each base or ship and the dates you were there, and any aircraft you worked on.
  3. Read the list of possible presumptive matches.
  4. Tap a condition to see why it is listed: the exposure, the dates and the law.
  5. Add the ones you have to your claim.

Questions this answers

What is the PACT Act?
The PACT Act is a law that expands VA health care and benefits for veterans exposed to burn pits, Agent Orange, and other toxic substances. It added more than 20 presumptive conditions, added presumptive locations for Agent Orange and radiation, and requires a toxic exposure screening for every veteran enrolled in VA health care.Source: The PACT Act and your VA benefits (VA.gov)
What conditions are presumptive for burn pit exposure?
VA lists cancers including brain, gastrointestinal, glioblastoma, genitourinary, head, hematologic and lymphatic, lymphoma, melanoma, neck, pancreatic, reproductive, and respiratory cancers. It also lists illnesses including asthma diagnosed after service, chronic bronchitis, COPD, chronic rhinitis, chronic sinusitis, constrictive or obliterative bronchiolitis, emphysema, granulomatous disease, interstitial lung disease, pleuritis, pulmonary fibrosis, and sarcoidosis.Source: Exposure to burn pits and other specific environmental hazards (VA.gov)
Where do I need to have served to be presumed exposed to burn pits?
On or after August 2, 1990: Bahrain, Iraq, Kuwait, Oman, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, Somalia, the UAE, and certain waters including the Persian Gulf and Red Sea. On or after September 11, 2001: Afghanistan, Djibouti, Egypt, Jordan, Lebanon, Syria, Uzbekistan, and Yemen. The airspace above these countries also counts.Source: Exposure to burn pits and other specific environmental hazards (VA.gov)
Where was Agent Orange exposure presumed?
VA presumes exposure for service in the Republic of Vietnam, on its inland waterways, or within 12 nautical miles offshore from January 9, 1962, to May 7, 1975. The PACT Act added U.S. or Royal Thai bases in Thailand, Laos, parts of Cambodia, Guam or American Samoa, and Johnston Atoll for set dates, and service in or near the Korean DMZ from September 1, 1967, to August 31, 1971, also qualifies.Source: Agent Orange exposure and disability compensation (VA.gov)
What are the Agent Orange presumptive conditions?
Cancers on the list include bladder cancer, chronic B-cell leukemia, Hodgkin's disease, multiple myeloma, non-Hodgkin's lymphoma, prostate cancer, respiratory cancers, and some soft tissue sarcomas. Other conditions include type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure, hypothyroidism, ischemic heart disease, MGUS, Parkinson's disease, parkinsonism, AL amyloidosis, and, with timing rules, chloracne, early-onset peripheral neuropathy, and porphyria cutanea tarda.Source: Agent Orange exposure and disability compensation (VA.gov)
Who qualifies for Camp Lejeune VA disability benefits?
You must have served at Camp Lejeune or MCAS New River, North Carolina, for at least 30 days total between August 1, 1953, and December 31, 1987, without a dishonorable discharge, and have a diagnosis of one of 8 presumptive conditions: adult leukemia, aplastic anemia and other myelodysplastic syndromes, bladder cancer, kidney cancer, liver cancer, multiple myeloma, non-Hodgkin's lymphoma, or Parkinson's disease.Source: Camp Lejeune water contamination health issues (VA.gov)
Do I need a lawyer to get VA benefits for Camp Lejeune?
No. You do not need to hire a lawyer or file a lawsuit to get VA benefits, including benefits related to Camp Lejeune. Filing for relief under the Camp Lejeune Justice Act does not affect your eligibility for VA disability or health care.Source: Camp Lejeune water contamination health issues (VA.gov)
VA denied my claim before, but my condition is now presumptive. What do I do?
File a Supplemental Claim, and VA will review your case again. You do not have to wait for VA to contact you first.Source: The PACT Act and your VA benefits (VA.gov)
What records do I need to prove toxic exposure?
For Agent Orange claims, VA asks for a medical record showing the condition and military records showing your time and location of service, such as your DD214. For C-123 aircraft claims, VA also lists USAF Forms 2096, 5, or 781 as supporting records.Source: Agent Orange exposure and disability compensation (VA.gov)
What is the VA toxic exposure screening?
Every veteran enrolled in VA health care gets an initial toxic exposure screening and a follow-up at least once every 5 years. It asks about exposures like burn pits, Gulf War hazards, Agent Orange, radiation, and Camp Lejeune water, then points you to related benefits and registry exams.Source: The PACT Act and your VA benefits (VA.gov)
