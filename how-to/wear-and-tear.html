How to claim wear and tear from what you did in service

A job code misses most of what service put your body through. Wear and Tear starts from the activity instead, across 226 of them, and lists the conditions published research links to each, the records that prove you did it, and questions for your own statement.

Where it is in the app: Claim Intel tab, Wear and Tear view. Part of VA Ready Pro, with a free preview.

Steps

  1. Open the Claim Intel tab and switch to Wear and Tear.
  2. Search an activity (ruck, doors, jumps) or browse the categories.
  3. Open an activity and mark I did this. The conditions from everything you mark gather into one ranked list.
  4. Read How to prove you did this for the records that show it.
  5. Answer the statement questions, then copy or share your statement, or ask a buddy for one.

Questions this answers

Can I get VA disability for injuries from training, like rucking or parachute jumps?
Yes, if the evidence shows a current disability, an injury or event during service, and a link between them. VA must weigh the places, types, and circumstances of your service along with all medical and lay evidence, not only formal sick call records.Source: 38 CFR 3.303
How do I prove something happened in service if it is not in my records?
You can submit lay evidence: written statements from you or from people who know about your condition or the events, such as service members who served with you, family, or friends. VA reviews lay evidence along with your other evidence.Source: Evidence needed for your disability claim (VA.gov)
Does VA accept my own statement about what I experienced?
Yes, for things a layperson can observe and describe. VA defines competent lay evidence as evidence from a person with knowledge of the facts who describes matters a layperson can observe, without needing specialized training.Source: 38 CFR 3.159
What if my injury happened in combat and was never documented?
For combat veterans, satisfactory lay or other evidence that an injury or disease was incurred or aggravated in combat is accepted as proof if it is consistent with the circumstances, conditions, or hardships of that service, even without an official record.Source: 38 CFR 3.304
What does benefit of the doubt mean for my claim?
When the positive and negative evidence on a point is roughly balanced, VA must resolve the doubt in your favor. The rule applies even without official records, especially when the incident arose in combat or similarly strenuous conditions.Source: 38 CFR 3.102
Do I need a medical opinion to connect my condition to service?
Usually. VA says it usually needs medical records or medical opinions to support the link between your current condition and what happened in service, and it may also accept lay evidence like a buddy statement.Source: Evidence needed for your disability claim (VA.gov)
How bad does my hearing need to be for VA to count it as hearing loss?
VA counts hearing loss as a disability when your threshold at any of 500, 1000, 2000, 3000, or 4000 Hz is 40 decibels or more, when at least three of those frequencies are 26 decibels or more, or when your Maryland CNC speech recognition score is below 94 percent.Source: 38 CFR 3.385
I had joint pain in service. Is my arthritis now automatically service connected?
Not automatically. Isolated findings like joint pain in service do not by themselves establish later arthritis. You can still get service connection for a condition diagnosed after discharge when all the evidence, including evidence about your service, shows it began in service.Source: 38 CFR 3.303
What if my service records were destroyed in the 1973 St. Louis fire?
The 1973 fire at the National Personnel Records Center destroyed records for some Army and Air Force veterans discharged during certain periods. If your records were lost, VA can help you reconstruct them to support your claim.Source: Evidence needed for your disability claim (VA.gov)
