VA Disability for 25U (Signal Support Systems Specialist)

Army 25U (Signal Support Systems Specialist) is a job that typically involved hazardous noise, heavy loads and impact, repetitive strain, vibration, and posture, and radiation. That pattern is why veterans who held it commonly claim conditions like back or neck strain, tinnitus, hearing loss, and limited shoulder motion. This page covers the general pattern for the job, not your record.

Typical exposures for 25U

These are the general hazards that came with the job. Your own exposure depends on your units, deployments, and duties.

  • Hazardous noise

    Weapons fire, engines, aircraft, generators, or machinery at levels linked to tinnitus and hearing loss.

  • Heavy loads and impact

    Rucks, body armor, lifting, carrying patients or ammunition, and hard landings that wear on the back, knees, and feet.

  • Repetitive strain, vibration, and posture

    The same motion thousands of times, long hours seated in vehicles or at a console, or constant vibration from vehicles and tools.

  • Radiation

    Ionizing radiation sources, or radio-frequency energy from radar and transmitters, depending on the duty. About this exposure →

PACT Act presumptives for 25U veterans

Many soldiers in this job deployed to Southwest Asia or the post-9/11 theater. Under the PACT Act, the presumption is based on where and when you served, not on your job title. It covers Gulf War service on or after August 2, 1990 in places such as Bahrain, Iraq, Kuwait, Oman, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, Somalia, and the United Arab Emirates, and post-9/11 service on or after September 11, 2001 in places such as Afghanistan, Djibouti, Egypt, Jordan, Lebanon, Syria, Uzbekistan, and Yemen.

If you served in a qualifying location and are later diagnosed, the VA treats certain conditions as presumed service-connected, so you do not have to prove the exposure caused them. The list includes asthma diagnosed after service, chronic bronchitis, COPD, chronic rhinitis, chronic sinusitis, constrictive bronchiolitis, and a range of cancers. Holding this job does not by itself mean you have any of them.

Common questions

Does the VA have a list of conditions for 25U?

No. The VA does not keep a list of disabilities by MOS, AFSC, or rating. It decides each claim on your own service records, medical evidence, and exam. What a job typically exposed people to can help explain how a condition started, which is why knowing the pattern for 25U Signal Support Systems Specialist is useful.

What conditions do 25U veterans commonly claim?

Veterans who served as Army 25U (Signal Support Systems Specialist) commonly claim conditions such as back or neck strain, tinnitus, hearing loss, limited shoulder motion, migraines, and limited knee flexion. Each one still needs its own diagnosis and a link to service.

Are 25U veterans covered by the PACT Act?

The PACT Act presumptions are based on where and when you served, not on your job. If you served in a qualifying Gulf War or post-9/11 location, certain respiratory conditions and cancers are presumed service-connected.
